Solutions

What exists to keep people working safely in heat, where each approach stops working, and where it is in use. Solutions are grouped by cooling approach. Maturity is lab → pilot → commercial.

Work scheduling & rest–shade–water

Organisational

Shift the work out of the heat: midday stoppages, rotation, mandated breaks.

Works because
  • Costs nothing to specify
  • Effective in every sector
  • Basis of most regulation
Limits
  • Collides with deadlines and daylight
  • Compliance is hard to verify
  • No help when the task cannot wait
Current applications
  • Spain and Greece midday bans
  • Gulf states' Jun–Sep bans
  • OSHA National Emphasis Program inspections
Commercial

Evaporative & phase-change vests

Personal

Water or PCM packs worn on the torso, absorbing heat as they change phase.

Works because
  • Cheap and widely available
  • No power required
  • Well understood by buyers
Limits
  • Finite capacity, then it is ballast
  • Evaporative fails in high humidity
  • Recharge needs a freezer or cold store
Current applications
  • Widely issued on European construction sites
  • Motorsport pit crews
  • Warehouse and foundry work
Commercial

Thermoelectric (TEC) cooling

Personal

Solid-state Peltier modules moving heat electrically, with no refrigerant loop.

Works because
  • Solid state, no moving parts
  • Small and light
  • Continuous while powered
Limits
  • Low efficiency at real thermal loads
  • Rated cooling rarely published
  • Rejects heat immediately next to the body
Current applications
  • Consumer and prosumer vests
  • Industrial pilots, few published results
Pilot

Vapour-compression refrigeration (VCR)

Personal

A real refrigeration cycle, miniaturised to be worn — the same physics as a fridge.

Works because
  • Highest cooling capacity per watt
  • Works independent of humidity
  • Can hold a set temperature
Limits
  • Heaviest and most complex
  • Needs a battery budget
  • Refrigerant choice is a regulatory question
Current applications
  • Early industrial and defence pilots
  • No certified operating envelope published yet
Pilot

Liquid-cooled garments

Personal

Tubing circulating chilled liquid, fed by a vehicle loop or a carried chiller.

Works because
  • Very effective where a loop exists
  • Long established in defence and motorsport
  • Comfortable under armour
Limits
  • Tethered, or the chiller is carried
  • Bulk and connector failure points
  • Poor fit for roaming work
Current applications
  • Armoured vehicle crews
  • Formula and endurance racing
  • EVA suits and hazmat entry
Commercial

Passive & radiative fabrics

Material

Textiles engineered to emit infrared through the atmospheric window, with no power.

Works because
  • Works unpowered
  • Maintenance-free once issued
  • Scales as ordinary clothing
Limits
  • Single-digit degrees at best
  • Needs sky view to work
  • Mostly still lab-stage
Current applications
  • Early architectural and tenting products
  • Field trials in agriculture
Lab

Site-level cooling

Environmental

Cool the place, not the person: shade structures, misting, cooled rest areas.

Works because
  • Helps everyone at once
  • No wearer-compliance problem
  • Doubles as a rest facility
Limits
  • Fixed in place while the work moves
  • Water and energy hungry
  • Capital cost per site
Current applications
  • Mandated rest areas on Gulf megaprojects
  • Misting in Mediterranean agriculture
  • Cooling centres in US and Indian cities
Commercial

Method

Every claim is either sourced to a named primary or marked as inference. Heat-index and mortality figures are date-checked against the originating agency, because prior-year figures resurface constantly in secondary coverage. Two figures published earlier this week failed that check and are corrected above.

Cadence

Rebuilt daily. Weekly consolidation on Saturday.

Limits

Mortality figures here are all-cause temperature-attributable estimates, not counts of people certified as dying of heat. They describe the population backdrop. Forward-looking mortality projections are modelled forecasts, not observations. Occupational exposure figures come separately from ETUI and EU-OSHA. Heat-mortality surveillance quality varies significantly by country; where no reconciled national series exists, that gap is reported explicitly rather than omitted.
