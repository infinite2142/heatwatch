Heat extremes & records

August 2026 ties the all-time monthly record

Copernicus (C3S) reports August 2026 tied July 2023 as the hottest calendar month in the instrumental record, with global-mean surface air temperature roughly 1.65 °C above the pre-industrial baseline — again above the 1.5 °C marker for an individual month. Western, central and south-eastern Europe and southern Scandinavia ran significantly drier than normal across the summer, consistent with the wildfire and excess-death season already on the record.

So whatA single month above 1.5 °C is not a breach of the long-term commitment. It is, however, the ambient condition outdoor work now happens in.

Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S)

Spain closes its hottest summer on record — on duration, not just peaks

Spain ran 61 days under heatwave conditions in summer 2026, against a previous record of 41 in 2022, and recorded its hottest summer in the series. The Balearics saw their warmest summer since records began in 1961, at a mean 27.1 °C (+3 °C anomaly). Heat-attributed deaths reached ~5,232 between 15 May and 31 August (ISCIII/MoMo).

So whatDuration is the number that matters for work. A single 45 °C afternoon is a forecast problem; 61 days is a staffing, scheduling and liability problem.

AEMET season close-out · ISCIII/MoMo · Euronews/Reuters, 9 Sep 2026

The Mediterranean loaded both hazards this season

The same record Mediterranean sea-surface temperatures that extended the summer heat drove the mid-September flash-flood sequence. The heat-to-DANA handover ran from forecast, to orange alert, to a live event across the Valencia region, to a red alert on the Murcia coast with one death at Olivella, and back down again — inside a single week. The toll held at one; the October 2024 reference event was revised to 232 deaths on 3 September, some 22 months on.

So whatHeat and flood are one loaded season rather than two separate stories, and both land on the same outdoor workforce.

Fire

European season closed; no major active event in window

No major active European wildfire event in the current window. For season context: July 2026 wildfires forced more than 250,000 evacuations across France and Spain, and the summer ran significantly drier than normal across western, central and south-eastern Europe.

So whatFire exposure is a heat-and-smoke exposure problem for outdoor and emergency workers, not only a land-loss problem.

EU Quality Jobs Act — the live one

The second-phase social-partner consultation naming extreme heat under safety and health at work is open to 28 September 2026, with the parallel call for evidence and impact-assessment feedback running to 29 September. Commission adoption is planned for Q4 2026. The union campaign (EFFAT, EPSU, EFBWW under #TooHotToWork) is pressing for binding WBGT cessation thresholds — 30 °C for high-intensity work, 32.5 °C for low-intensity — plus risk assessments, paid cooling breaks and employer sanctions.

European Commission, Employment and Social Affairs · ETUC

United StatesNEP renewed to April 2031; permanent rule stalled

United States — enforcement firm, standard still stalled

The federal heat National Emphasis Program was renewed on 10 April 2026 and runs through April 2031, expanded to 55 high-risk industries. The permanent federal standard remains stalled: OSHA has signalled further revisions to its August 2024 proposed rule, with a supplemental NPRM possible later in 2026. Enforcement runs through the NEP and the General Duty Clause. Cal/OSHA's revised state proposal is progressing.

Gulf — the longest-running rule, and its limits

The midday outdoor-work ban ran 15 June to 15 September for the 22nd consecutive year. Human Rights Watch found in June 2026 that these calendar-based protections fall short of WBGT-based practice — a fixed clock window does not track the actual heat load on a given day.

Method

Cadence
Rebuilt daily. Weekly consolidation on Saturday.
Sourcing
Every claim is either sourced to a named primary or marked as inference. Heat-index and mortality figures are date-checked against the originating agency, because prior-year figures resurface constantly in secondary coverage.
What these numbers are not
Mortality figures here are all-cause temperature-attributable estimates (the MoMo/EuroMomo method), not counts of workers who died of heat. They describe the population backdrop. Occupational exposure figures come separately from ETUI and EU-OSHA.
Who publishes this
HeatWatch is published by Groundsuit, which makes active cooling equipment for industrial workers.
