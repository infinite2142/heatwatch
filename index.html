Spain & Iberia

Spain's 2026 heat season is closing out

Spain's national weather agency describes the country as heading into a cooler pattern: the current warm spell — running 5 to 10 °C above normal, with the Guadalquivir valley near 40 °C — holds through the weekend before easing from Sunday 27 September as a cooling front arrives on Monday 28 September. Active heat alerts have been stepped down to the lowest level, covering only a handful of regions.

So what: This marks the practical close of Spain's record 2026 heat season — a season that ran 61 heatwave days, the longest on record.

Sources — AEMET (Spain's national weather agency)

Heat & climate

A quiet week on the beachhead — the record from earlier in the month still stands

No new Iberian, UK, or Gulf heat event was recorded this week beyond the seasonal wind-down in Spain. Copernicus (C3S) reported that August 2026 tied July 2023 as the hottest calendar month in the instrumental record, at roughly 1.65 °C above the pre-industrial baseline. That figure remains the most current top-line macro statistic available.

So what: A quiet week does not mean the underlying trend has paused. The most recent global record still stands, and the EU's extreme-heat consultation is now three days from closing.

Sources — Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S)

Heat & climate

Europe's 2026 wildfire season: below the record, above the average

The EU's Joint Research Centre puts the area burned by wildfires across Europe this season at 678,978 hectares as of 23 September — up from 668,035 hectares a week earlier, a normal rate of seasonal accrual rather than a new event. The current figure remains below 2025, the worst year on the JRC's record, but above the trailing 20-year average. For season context: July 2026 wildfires forced more than 250,000 evacuations across France and Spain.

So what: Fire exposure is a heat-and-smoke exposure problem for outdoor and emergency workers, not only a land-loss problem.

Sources — EU Joint Research Centre — current European wildfire situation

UK & Northern Europe

An unseasonal warm spell forecast for the UK, 28–30 September

Weather maps continue to point to parts of south-east England reaching around 29 °C between 28 and 30 September, well above seasonal norms, before more unsettled Atlantic weather returns. This has not yet been restated in a fresh Met Office bulletin closer to the date.

So what: On its own this is a minor forecast item, but it fits the pattern behind the UK beachhead argument: 2026 was already the first year on record with 35 °C-plus readings in May, June and July. Heat exposure at work is increasingly a year-round scheduling question, not only a peak-summer one.

Sources — Met Office (via secondary press coverage)

Europe

EU Quality Jobs Act — three days to close

The second-phase social-partner consultation naming extreme heat under safety and health at work is now three days from its 28 September close, with the parallel call for evidence and impact-assessment feedback running to 29 September. Commission adoption is planned for Q4 2026. The union campaign (EFFAT, EPSU, EFBWW under #TooHotToWork) continues pressing for binding WBGT cessation thresholds — 30 °C for high-intensity work, 32.5 °C for low-intensity — plus risk assessments, paid cooling breaks and employer sanctions.

Sources — European Commission, Employment and Social Affairs · ETUC

United States

United States — enforcement program firm, permanent rule pushed to late 2027

An OSHA official said on 16 September that the agency may issue a supplemental proposal on heat in the coming months, but that a final rule is not expected until late 2027 per the agency's own regulatory agenda — later than earlier reporting had suggested. The federal heat National Emphasis Program, renewed 10 April 2026, runs through April 2031 and covers 55 high-risk industries, and is unaffected by the permanent rule's timeline.

Sources — OSHA Directorate of Standards and Guidance, 16 Sep 2026 (NSC Safety Congress & Expo) · OSHA.gov, 10 Apr 2026

Gulf

Gulf — season closed, confirmed

The UAE's Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation marked the formal conclusion of the 2026 Occupational Heat Stress Prevention Policy on 15 September — the 22nd consecutive year of the midday outdoor-work ban, with more than 12,000 worker rest stations deployed across the summer. Human Rights Watch found in June 2026 that these calendar-based protections still fall short of WBGT-based practice.

Sources — MoHRE · Human Rights Watch, Jun 2026

Method

Every claim is either sourced to a named primary or marked as inference. Heat-index and mortality figures are date-checked against the originating agency, because prior-year figures resurface constantly in secondary coverage.

Cadence

Rebuilt daily. Weekly consolidation on Saturday.

Limits

Mortality figures here are all-cause temperature-attributable estimates (the MoMo/EuroMomo method), not counts of workers who died of heat. They describe the population backdrop. Occupational exposure figures come separately from ETUI and EU-OSHA.
