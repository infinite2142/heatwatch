Heat extremes & records

A quiet week on the beachhead — the record from ten days ago still stands

No new Iberian, UK, or Gulf heat event was recorded in the past several days. Copernicus (C3S) reported on 10 September that August 2026 tied July 2023 as the hottest calendar month in the instrumental record, at roughly 1.65 °C above the pre-industrial baseline. That figure remains the most current top-line macro statistic available.

So whatA quiet week does not mean the underlying trend has paused. The most recent global record is ten days old and still stands, and the EU's extreme-heat consultation is now five days from closing.

Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S)

An unseasonal warm spell forecast for the UK, 28–30 September

The Met Office forecasts parts of south-east England reaching around 29 °C between 28 and 30 September, well above seasonal norms, before more unsettled Atlantic weather returns.

So whatOn its own this is a minor forecast item, but it fits the pattern behind the UK beachhead argument: 2026 was already the first year on record with 35 °C-plus readings in May, June and July. Heat exposure at work is increasingly a year-round scheduling question, not only a peak-summer one.

Met Office (via Karmactive)

Spain's record summer, still the reference point

Spain ran 61 days under heatwave conditions in summer 2026, against a previous record of 41 in 2022, and recorded its hottest summer on record. Heat-attributed deaths reached approximately 5,232 between 15 May and 31 August (ISCIII/MoMo).

So whatDuration, not just peak temperature, is what drives staffing, scheduling and liability exposure for employers of outdoor and industrial workers.

EU Quality Jobs Act — five days to close

The second-phase social-partner consultation naming extreme heat under safety and health at work is now five days from its 28 September close, with the parallel call for evidence and impact-assessment feedback running to 29 September. Commission adoption is planned for Q4 2026. The union campaign (EFFAT, EPSU, EFBWW under #TooHotToWork) continues pressing for binding WBGT cessation thresholds — 30 °C for high-intensity work, 32.5 °C for low-intensity — plus risk assessments, paid cooling breaks and employer sanctions.

European Commission, Employment and Social Affairs · ETUC

United StatesNEP runs to April 2031; permanent rule stalled

United States — enforcement firm, a supplemental proposal may be near

The federal heat National Emphasis Program, renewed 10 April 2026, runs through April 2031 and covers 55 high-risk industries. The permanent federal standard remains stalled since its August 2024 proposal. Trade press has reported an OSHA staffer saying a supplemental proposed rule on heat could appear soon, though as of this report nothing has been filed with the Federal Register.

OSHA.gov, 10 Apr 2026 · Safety+Health Magazine

Gulf — season closed, confirmed

The UAE's Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation marked the formal conclusion of the 2026 Occupational Heat Stress Prevention Policy on 15 September — the 22nd consecutive year of the midday outdoor-work ban, with more than 12,000 worker rest stations deployed across the summer. Human Rights Watch found in June 2026 that these calendar-based protections still fall short of WBGT-based practice.

Method

Cadence
Rebuilt daily. Weekly consolidation on Saturday.
Sourcing
Every claim is either sourced to a named primary or marked as inference. Heat-index and mortality figures are date-checked against the originating agency, because prior-year figures resurface constantly in secondary coverage.
What these numbers are not
Mortality figures here are all-cause temperature-attributable estimates (the MoMo/EuroMomo method), not counts of workers who died of heat. They describe the population backdrop. Occupational exposure figures come separately from ETUI and EU-OSHA.
Who publishes this
HeatWatch is published by Groundsuit, which makes active cooling equipment for industrial workers.
