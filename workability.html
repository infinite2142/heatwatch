What is WBGT, and why not just the air temperature?

WBGT is the wet-bulb globe temperature: one number combining the four things that decide whether a body can stay cool outdoors — humidity, radiant heat, air temperature and wind. Occupational heat standards are written against it, ISO 7243 among them, which is why this page counts hours in WBGT rather than in degrees of air temperature.

Air temperature on its own is a poor guide. 30°C in dry shade with a breeze and 30°C in humid air under open sun are the same forecast and a different day's work. The body sheds heat by sweating, and sweat stops evaporating once the air is already wet — so humidity, not heat, is usually what makes outdoor work unsafe.

That is why the terms are weighted the way they are:

  • Natural wet bulb70%A thermometer with a wet sleeve, left in the open air. It measures how well sweat can evaporate — which is how a body actually sheds heat. It falls as the air gets drier and windier, and rises as it gets more humid.
  • Globe20%A matt black sphere the size of a grapefruit. It measures radiant heat, which outdoors is mostly the sun. This is the term that makes shade worth having.
  • Air temperature10%The ordinary dry-bulb reading a weather forecast gives you — the smallest part of the answer.

The limit is not a single number either. Harder work produces more heat, and a worker not yet used to heat has less room before it becomes dangerous, so ISO 7243 sets six of them — from 30°C for light work by an acclimatised worker down to 22°C for heavy work by someone new to it. The Workload and Workers controls above switch between the six.

What this is not. These hours are computed from a public weather forecast for each hub's location. They are not a reading taken on your site, where shade, surfaces, enclosure and the work itself all move the number, and they are not an instruction to stop work. What the law requires is in the rule column beside them, and it comes from dated records, not from this forecast.

United Arab Emirates prohibits outdoor work in direct sunlight between 12:30 and 15:00 from 15 June to 15 September each year. Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation midday break, in its 22nd year. The ban is a fixed calendar window, so it does not respond to heat outside those dates.

Qatar — midday outdoor work ban
Ministerial Decision No. (17) of 2021

Qatar prohibits outdoor work in direct sunlight between 10:00 and 15:30 from 1 June to 15 September each year, the widest window in the Gulf. Separately, Ministerial Decision 17 of 2021 requires all work to stop whenever the wet-bulb globe temperature exceeds 32.1 C, which unlike the calendar ban responds to the actual conditions.

Singapore — heat stress measures for outdoor work

Singapore's Ministry of Manpower sets rest breaks against measured WBGT: at least 10 minutes an hour for heavy work at 32 C and above, and 15 minutes at 33 C. WBGT meters have been required since 2024 at larger worksites, shipyards and in process manufacturing. Four further measures become mandatory on 1 December 2026.

How this page is made

Method

Forecast

Hourly forecasts from the Open-Meteo API, licensed CC BY 4.0, stored as issued and never revised afterwards.

Heat stress

Wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) combines humidity, radiant heat, air temperature and wind into the measure occupational standards are written against; it is explained in full under This week. It is solved hour by hour after Liljegren et al. (2008) from temperature, humidity, wind, pressure and the direct and diffuse solar components. Shade is the same model with the direct beam removed, not a fixed offset. Limits are the ISO 7243:2017 reference values for the workload and acclimatisation selected.

Rules

Every rule shown comes from a dated record with at least one source. A record that has not been confirmed against a primary source is labelled as such and is not counted as a rule in force. Rule summaries are not legal advice.

Archive

Each day's forecast, computed hours and rule status are written once and listed in a published manifest of SHA-256 hashes, so a past day can be shown to be unchanged.

Limits

The hours are a forecast of heat stress, not an official instruction to stop work, and they are computed for a location rather than a site. Employers keep their own records; this page does not hold them.

HeatWatch is published by Groundsuit, which makes active cooling equipment for industrial workers.
